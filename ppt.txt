Part 1: Problem Definition & Market Context

Q: What problem are you trying to solve?
A: Alert noise drowns out critical incidents — manual triage is slow, inconsistent, and burns out SOC/SRE teams.

Q: Please define the identified business need and importance to business.
A: Automated triage reduces MTTR, eliminates alert fatigue, and ensures critical incidents reach the right engineer — directly protecting service uptime SLAs.

Q: Who is affected by this problem? What improvements are assumed to be brought by implementation?
A: SOC analysts, SRE teams, and incident responders. Implementation brings faster triage, traceable audits, reduced noise, and automated escalation.

Q: What made you choose this problem?
A: Alert fatigue is a universal pain point — a deterministic, auditable approach (no ML) works in production SOC environments where predictability matters.

Q: Please elaborate any market research conducted help frame the problem.
A: Gartner and Ponemon cite alert fatigue as a top SOC challenge; our approach follows Google SRE model, NIST framework, and aligns with commercial SOC platform capabilities.

Part 2: Solution & Implementation

Q: Please provide short description of the solution.
A: A deterministic rule-based engine that ingests alerts, runs dual-track analysis (rules + risk scoring), detects false positives, and pages the right on-call engineer — all under a second.

Q: What are the key objectives you are planning to achieve?
A: 60%+ reduction in MTTR, eliminate false-positive noise, ensure every critical alert escalates instantly, and provide full audit trails for compliance.

Q: How does your solution achieve to address the problem statement?
A: Rules match alert types, risk scores quantify severity, FP signals suppress noise, and escalation logic maps to on-call engineers — fully automated with zero human intervention.

Q: Please explain the technology stack selected.
A: FastAPI + SQLAlchemy + SQLite/PostgreSQL + Jinja2 templates + vanilla JS/CSS + ReportLab (PDFs) + APScheduler — minimal, auditable, dependency-light.

Q: Please provide justification on why your approach is best.
A: Fully explainable decisions, zero hallucination risk, easy rule customization, no training data needed — unlike black-box ML, it's built for production auditability.

Q: What is the state you were able to achieve?
A: A working web app with alert ingestion, dual-track triage, FP detection, on-call assignment, runbook suggestions, PDF/CSV reports, and a real-time dashboard — tested with curated incident scenarios.

Part 4: Feature Breakdowns
Q: Please elaborate Feature1: Dual-Track Triage Engine.
A: Ingested alerts run through two parallel analysis tracks — a configurable rule engine matches alert type to conditions (field > threshold, pattern match) and assigns severity, while a weighted risk scorer computes a 0-120 score from high-signal metrics (requests/sec, latency, packet loss). Results are merged with dynamic severity winning if it's CRITICAL or HIGH.

Q: Please elaborate Feature2: False Positive Detection & Suppression.
A: A signal-based confidence scorer evaluates per-rule FP signals and heuristics (e.g., status == "healthy"). If confidence >= 50%, severity is overridden to LOW and escalation is fully suppressed — drastically reducing noise while keeping genuine threats visible.

Q: Please elaborate Feature3: Automated Escalation & Engineer Assignment.
A: Final severity maps to escalation levels (CRITICAL→SEV-1, HIGH→SEV-2, MEDIUM→SEV-3, LOW→NO-ESCALATION), matched against an on-call engineer roster by team and escalation level. A relevant runbook is suggested alongside each result for immediate responder action.
